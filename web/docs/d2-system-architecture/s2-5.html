Assumptions and Constraints

One of the key assumptions underlying the design and deployment of the Platform in the CopernicusLAC Centre is that the system will be deployed in an infrastructure that provides Kubernetes as a Service (KaaS) and Databases as a Service (DBaaS). This assumption has several implications for the system design, scalability, maintenance, and overall operational efficiency:

  • Kubernetes as a Service (KaaS): The use of KaaS implies that the management and orchestration of containerized applications are handled through a cloud service provider. This facilitates ease of deployment, scalability, and management of the system's applications and services. Kubernetes provides a framework for automating deployment, scaling, and operations of application containers across clusters of hosts.
  • Object storage as a Service: The use of object storage enables data management through a highly available S3 compatible API, allowing to benefit from a performant and scalable access to large amounts of unstructured data, from anywhere in the platform. This choice ensures that data retrieval is both swift, reliable and, in keeping with modern geospatial data practices, supports Cloud-Optimised Geotiffs (CoG). In addition to the primary object storage layer, the infrastructure expects also to interoperate with a deep/cold storage archive tier, such as those offered by commercial cloud providers. Data recovered from cold storage will be staged back into the primary S3-compatible object storage layer for access and processing. The associated access latencies and management workflows will be handled at the orchestration level.
  • Databases as a Service (DBaaS): The assumption that databases are provided as a service indicates that the data storage and management aspects of the system are hosted and managed by a dedicated provider. This service typically includes database setup, maintenance, backups, and scalability, thereby reducing the overhead on our system for these tasks. DBaaS ensures high availability, performance, and scalability of the database integral to the system. The preferred DBaaS technology is PostgreSQL, which is assumed to be available as a managed relational database service from the hosting provider. This choice is aligned with the implementation details provided in the component-specific architecture sections (3.2.4, 3.4.4, 3.6.4) and the technology stack outlined in Section 7.

Given these infrastructure assumptions, the system is designed with certain constraints and considerations:

  • Dependency on Resource Provider: Relying on Kubernetes as a service and databases as a service means the system's performance and availability are partly contingent on the reliability and capabilities of the provider.
  • Scalability and Resource Management: While these services provide scalability, there is a dependency on their ability to allocate resources. Any limitations in their scalability could impact the performance of the Platform services.
